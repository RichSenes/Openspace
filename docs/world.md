# The world

Openspace's home becomes one isometric world: a lab at the centre of a city, built from the
offices that are set up and moved by the work that really happens. The study is at `?lab=world`;
this page records what was decided for turning it into the real thing.

## What is decided

**The picture**

- Isometric, a little higher than Habbo's angle, dense pixel art painted in code.
- One open-plan lab: Shiori's wide area in the middle, an area per office round her, set off by
  glass partitions with an opening towards her, a lounge for everybody. Its plan is made from the
  setup: area sizes follow the number of agents, shapes follow the names, every agent has a seat
  they can walk to.
- The lab is always the centre of the screen. Around it a bright, untidy city: streets at uneven
  distances, buildings of several kinds, parks, traffic lights, cars and people. It is painted
  once, wide; resizing the page only moves the window onto it.
- Light: daylight outside by the hour and the weather; cold lamps inside that differ by area;
  pools of light thrown by screens, capsules, Shiori's well and street lamps; rain, lightning and
  rare stuttering lamps in a storm.
- Each office shows its name over its area.

**The people**

- One character per desk: always there, with their own character traits. Interns come in when
  needed and leave when done.
- One character per routine, living in the office the routine serves; general routines live
  next to Shiori.
- Roles show in the clothes: desk agents wear a white coat over a shirt of their office's colour,
  interns a short light-blue coat and a badge, routines a grey technician's overall. The sprites
  themselves will be redrawn from references.

**What they do**

- Working: at their desk, typing.
- Needing the person (a question, an approval, a task to review): they walk to Shiori and queue
  round her dome, each with an icon for what they want.
- Failed or blocked: they queue at Shiori too, downcast, with a red icon.
- No work: social life. Lounge, coffee, talking to each other according to their traits and
  relationships; the bubbles carry real small talk, generated by the local model sparingly and
  only while the graphics card is free.
- Work meetings: when two pieces of work touch (an agreement between offices, a task that
  unblocks another, the same files), the agents walk to each other and talk, and a meeting note
  is written to the brain, linked to both.

**Clicking**

- A character: a card opens over the world, without leaving it: who they are, what they are
  doing, the task and its last log lines, mood and relationships, and the actions (answer,
  approve, open the full page).
- Shiori: she wakes and you talk to her right there, with her voice and subtitles over the lab.
  Her card lists who is queuing and why; you answer them one by one, and each walks back to
  their desk.

## Steps

1. A live snapshot of the world from the backend: offices and desks (with traits), routines and
   the office each serves, interns, and for each character what they are doing now and whether
   they wait for the person.
2. The lab built from that snapshot instead of the demo setup, characters moved by it: desk work,
   the queue at Shiori, failures.
3. The character card and the queue at Shiori, with their actions.
4. Talking to Shiori inside the world (her voice and subtitles reused).
5. Social life with real small talk, rate-limited, on the local model.
6. Meeting notes when work touches.
7. The world as the home page, with a thin bar on top and a way to every other page; then the
   pages are reviewed one by one.

Still to come, independent of the steps above: Shiori's brain drawn for the repository (the study
can use a local reference picture that is not published), and the characters' sprites, once their
references are chosen.
